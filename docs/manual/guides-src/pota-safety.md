# Field safety
## Radial and whip tips carry kilovolts
Tip voltage, V RMS (1/4 wave vertical, 80 m, 8 ft up)
| Radials | 5 W | 100 W | 1 kW |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 1 | 416 | 1860 | 5880 |
| 2 | 208 | 930 | 2940 |
| 3 | 139 | 620 | 1960 |
| 4 | 104 | 465 | 1470 |

- Voltage scales as sqrt(power) and as 1/N radials.
  One radial at 100 W = four at legal limit.
- QRP is not safe: a dipole at 5 W has ~200 V at the tips
- Insulator at every tip; solder ball over the wire end;
  rope the tips off; a folded tail moves the high-voltage
  end to the fold
- Rows 1-3 are extrapolated; treat as order of magnitude
---
## Before touching the analyser
- Discharge the antenna and coax (short centre to
  shield): coiled coax on a dry day holds static
- Never connect to a transmitter output; no one
  transmits on or near the antenna under test
- SMA savers on both ports, wrench-snug; calibrate at
  the saver face
- The sweep radiates at a low level; pause when needed
## Site
- Look UP before extending a 17 ft whip: power lines
- Flag guy lines; they are a trip hazard
- Bleed static before touching the feedpoint in wind

Source: ARRL Antenna Book Vol 1 Fig 3.27; portvert s8.3
